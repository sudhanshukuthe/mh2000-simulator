Five apparatus from the lecture, driven by the same thermodynamic data as the slides. Change the settings, wait for equilibrium, and record your own data.

Sealed tube in a two-zone furnace: Au–Cd

after Bartha & Alexander, Can. J. Chem. 1965

What happens

Liquid Cd in the cold zone fixes the Cd pressure in the tube at p°Cd(T₁). The gold absorbs Cd until its own Cd pressure, aCd·p°Cd(T₂), is the same. Weigh the sample to get xCd.

Try this

  • Record a point at equilibrium, then change T₁ and record again to build the aCd–x curve.
  • Record before equilibrium and see how far off the point lands.

Transpiration: vapour pressure of liquid Cu

cf. Morris & Zellars, 1956 · 1450–1600 °C

What happens

Carrier gas picks up Cu vapour over the melt; the vapour condenses downstream. From the condensed mass and the gas volume, pCu = nCu/(nCu+ncarrier)·P.

Try this

  • Run at 5, 20, 80, 300 mL/min. Find the plateau.
  • At low flow, back-diffusion inflates the result; at high flow the gas leaves unsaturated. Compare Ar with He.

CO–CO₂ gas over graphite: controlled pO₂

ΔG°₄ = 170 700 − 174.5T J · ΔG°₃ = −564 800 + 173.62T J

What happens

With graphite present, C + CO₂ = 2CO sets the outlet gas whatever you feed in. Without graphite, the inlet CO/CO₂ ratio fixes pO₂ through 2CO + O₂ = 2CO₂.

Try this

  • Heat from 900 K to 1873 K with graphite in. Watch pCO₂ collapse to ~10⁻⁵ atm.
  • Remove graphite at 900 K and pick a CO-rich inlet: carbon deposition becomes favourable.

H₂ through a water bubbler: oxidise or reduce?

H₂ + ½O₂ = H₂O(g), ΔG° = −246 450 + 54.8T J

What happens

The bubbler temperature is the gas dew point and sets pH₂O. The H₂O/H₂ ratio then fixes pO₂ in the furnace. The sample oxidises if that ratio is above the metal/oxide equilibrium line.

Try this

  • Fe at 1273 K: find the dew point where FeO starts to form.
  • Switch to Cr: even very dry H₂ oxidises it. Ni never oxidises in this gas.

Impeller mixing: induced emulsification

after White & Sichen, MMTB 45B (2014) · 1823 K

What happens

While Ca transfers fast, the apparent interfacial tension is low and the impeller tears metal into droplets: the interfacial area jumps. As ΔG → 0 the tension recovers and the droplets recoalesce, even though mixing continues.

Try this

  • Run at 100 rpm and watch area peak, then fall.
  • Repeat at 0 and 50 rpm: equilibrium takes far longer without the emulsion.
